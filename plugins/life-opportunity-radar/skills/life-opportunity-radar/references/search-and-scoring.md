# Search and Scoring Protocol

Use this protocol to prevent trend summaries from masquerading as opportunity research.

## 1. Observation architecture

Maintain conceptual coverage equivalent to **12 permanent observation surfaces plus 6 rotating surfaces**. These are coverage roles, not a requirement to cite exactly 18 websites in every answer.

Permanent coverage:

1. frontier capability changes;
2. open-source releases and developer adoption;
3. model/tool unit economics;
4. evaluation and benchmark gaps;
5. agent reliability and observability;
6. security and permissions;
7. standards and interoperability;
8. data and synthetic environments;
9. distribution and permissionless publishing;
10. real buyer or employer demand;
11. product launches and failures;
12. China-specific access, regulation and market conditions.

Choose six rotating surfaces from the candidate pool stated in the current conversation or a user-provided private profile. Replace a rotating surface when it repeatedly produces no new capability, buyer evidence, accessible entry, or falsifying signal.

## 2. Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. official documentation, standards, repositories, release notes and regulatory text;
2. primary research papers with methods, data or code;
3. real pricing, job specifications, procurement, customer cases and public usage data;
4. identifiable builders describing actual implementation, including failures;
5. reputable analysis that links back to evidence;
6. media, social posts and aggregators only as lead generators.

When a source has incentives to market the claim, seek independent evidence. A GitHub repository proves code exists, not that users pay. A job description proves one employer seeks a role, not a broad market. Funding proves investor belief, not personal accessibility.

## 3. Scan sequence

1. Extract the seed and classify it as capability, demand, entry gate, infrastructure gap, regulation, distribution, economics, or possible falsifier.
2. Check the known-path list, if one was supplied. Identify what would actually be novel rather than a renamed familiar direction.
3. Search current primary sources in parallel across the most relevant permanent and rotating surfaces.
4. Trace strong claims to the underlying artifact.
5. Look deliberately for failure evidence, closed gates, incumbents, commoditization and buyer resistance.
6. Stop when the conclusion is supported or when additional results only repeat the same evidence. Do not pretend to exhaust the world.
7. State what the scan changes in the current direction set.

For a daily pulse, emphasize events from the last 7–30 days and use older sources only for necessary context. For a deep strategic scan, widen the horizon but verify that the opportunity still exists now.

## 4. Candidate scorecard

Use qualitative `LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH` ratings unless enough comparable evidence justifies numbers.

- **Personal mechanism fit:** compatibility with the user's stated strengths, energy patterns and preferred work mode; otherwise `UNKNOWN`.
- **Permissionless entry:** ability to begin and prove value without degree, employer, capital or relationships.
- **Three-year compounding asset:** code, data, benchmark, reputation, IP, distribution, workflow or certified expertise.
- **Upside:** plausible value if excellent, not the industry's total market size.
- **AI leverage:** whether improving AI makes the individual more capable rather than obsolete.
- **Social and energy fit:** required selling, meetings, management and customer support compared with stated preferences.
- **Financial and physical downside:** capital at risk, recurring API costs, liability and health burden.
- **Evidence maturity:** demonstrated demand and working systems versus research/demo speculation.
- **Global portability:** usefulness across countries, platforms and employers.

Never average away a fatal gate. Mark it separately.

## 5. Direction states

- `WATCH`: real signal, but entry or demand is not yet established.
- `PROBE`: enough evidence for a bounded proof-of-work or buyer check.
- `BET CANDIDATE`: repeated evidence of fit, accessible entry and compounding value; deserves serious time allocation discussion.
- `REJECT`: contradicted by user constraints, closed permission gates, weak ownership, unacceptable downside or failed evidence.

These states describe evidence, not destiny. A hot market may remain `WATCH`; a small open-source niche may become `BET CANDIDATE` if the personal entry is unusually strong.

## 6. Novelty test

Before calling anything an information-gap discovery, answer:

- Did the user already name or understand this direction?
- Is this merely a new label for an option already present in the current conversation or profile?
- Is the discovery actually a different value layer, permission mechanism, buyer, compounding asset or technical role?

If novelty is weak, say so and use the information only to update an existing path.

## 7. Output templates

### Pulse scan

For each of three to five signals:

```text
Signal:
Current evidence:
What is actually new:
Possible user position:
Entry gate:
Main trap or falsifier:
State: WATCH | PROBE | BET CANDIDATE | REJECT
Ranking effect:
```

### Deep scan

Include mechanism, current primary evidence, buyer/value flow, required capability stack, permission route, three-year asset, downside, fit evidence, unknowns, falsifier and next high-information proof.

### Comparison

Compare candidates using the scorecard, show fatal gates, and recommend an allocation or ordering only when evidence supports it. Do not select a winner by adding speculative numerical scores.
